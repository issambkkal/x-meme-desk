# Three hits in 48 hours: an election, an AI crash and a hot jobs report drained crypto

Bitcoin went into the US midterms near $80,000. Three days later it was at about $75,500,
more than $1 billion of leveraged bets had been wiped out, and the Fear & Greed index had
fallen from 50 to 28 in a day.

No single villain did it. It was a chain: a political result spooked the AI trade, the AI
trade dragged down crypto, and a strong jobs report took away the one thing that could have
cushioned the fall — cheaper money. Meme coins sat at the very end of that chain, which is
where the pain always lands hardest.

## What happened (the numbers)

- **Tue Nov 3, the election.** Democrats won both chambers of Congress. CBS projects
  Democratic control of the Senate from January after upsets in Texas, South Carolina and
  Kansas; NBC's tracker showed 54–44 with two seats still open. Bitcoin dipped to about
  $75,600 on election night and then bounced back above $80,000 (Decrypt).
- **Thu Nov 5, the AI crash.** The Nasdaq fell about 5.5%, its worst day since April 2025
  (CNBC, CNN). Nvidia lost about 9% and AMD 17.3%; CNN put the damage at roughly
  $2.5 trillion of market value. Axios said the index fell into correction territory.
- **Same day, crypto followed.** Bitcoin dropped about 6% to roughly $75,700 and gave back
  its whole post-election bounce (The Block). CoinGlass data showed $1.08 billion of
  liquidations in 24 hours, $954 million of it longs (crypto.news). Solana fell 6.3% to
  $89.90 (Crypto Times).
- **ETF money left.** US spot Bitcoin ETFs lost $430.2 million on Nov 5, with not one fund
  taking money in (Farside). That was the third outflow day in four: −$398.6M on Nov 2,
  −$115.6M on Nov 3, +$74.6M on Nov 4.
- **Fri Nov 6, the jobs report.** The US added 165,000 jobs in October against about
  65,000 expected, and unemployment dipped to 4.1% (CNBC, Benzinga). The 2-year Treasury
  yield rose to about 4.53%, the 10-year to about 4.87%, and the dollar index gained 0.6%
  to 102.1 (Bloomberg, ActionForex).

## Why it happened (the chain)

**Step 1: politics hit the AI trade.** Fox Business and Axios both tied Thursday's tech
crash to the Democratic sweep: investors started pricing tighter AI rules and slower data
center buildouts (Axios noted the incoming Texas governor had floated a moratorium on new
ones). Qualcomm's weak guidance poured fuel on it.

**Step 2: crypto traded like a tech stock.** Several outlets (CoinDesk, The Block,
Crypto Times) tied Bitcoin's drop directly to the chip selloff. Crypto trades all night and
is easy to sell when other markets hurt, and leverage did the rest:
in the four hours of the drop alone, The Block counted $880 million in liquidations,
$819 million of them longs.

**Step 3: no rescue from the Fed.** In a normal panic, traders hope for rate cuts. Not this
time. The Fed hiked in September and sits at 3.75%–4.00%, and Friday's 165,000 jobs pushed
the odds of another quarter-point hike on Dec 9 to roughly 75–80% (CME FedWatch via Invezz,
Morningstar and Reuters; the number moved during the day). Higher yields and a firmer
dollar make a 0% yielding asset like Bitcoin — and a 0% yielding dog coin — less attractive.

**Step 4: the crypto bill is in the freezer.** The CLARITY Act, the market-structure bill
the industry wanted, failed a Senate vote 49–50 on Sept 15 — 11 short of the 60 needed
(FinTech Weekly, DLA Piper). With Democrats taking the Senate, Elizabeth Warren is expected
to chair the Banking Committee in January (Crypto Times, citing the WSJ). Analysts expect the
bill to be rewritten from scratch. That doesn't change prices tomorrow, but it removes a
reason to buy "for the regulation pump".

## What the on-chain data shows

- **Meme coins fell harder than Bitcoin.** Dogecoin traded near $0.072, close to its 52-week
  low, down 9.6% in 7 days (CoinGecko data via The Coin Republic; CoinDesk price page).
- **The launchpad slowed.** DefiLlama shows Pump.fun revenue of about $7.5 million for
  Nov 1–6, against $62.4 million for all of October. That is about $1.26M a day versus
  about $2.0M a day in October: a pace roughly 37% slower.
- **Pump.fun kept selling.** It sent 76,111 SOL ($6.85M) to Kraken on Nov 6, taking its
  total sold since 2024 to about 5.55M SOL, or $882M (Lookonchain).
- **The dry powder didn't leave.** Circle minted about $750 million USDC on Solana in 24
  hours on Nov 5–6 (SolanaFloor), and total stablecoin supply was roughly flat near
  $289 billion (CoinGabbar). Money moved to the sidelines rather than out of crypto.
- **Big holders sold ETH.** Fidelity moved 27,000 ETH (about $60M) to Galaxy Digital on
  Nov 6 (OnchainLens).

## Who won, who lost

**Lost:** leveraged longs (about $954M in a day), late buyers of the election bounce, and
anyone in small-cap meme coins, which have no ETF bid and no buyback underneath them.
**Didn't lose:** shorts, stablecoin holders, and the platforms that take a fee on every
trade either way. **Bigger picture:** the Fear & Greed index fell to 28 ("fear") from 50 a
day earlier (Alternative.me via FearGreedMeter). Fear readings are not a buy signal on
their own; they just mean the easy money has already left.

## What to watch next (Doha time)

- **Tue Nov 10, 16:30 — US CPI for October.** September was 3.7% a year. Another hot print
  would make the December hike close to certain.
- **Fri Nov 13, 16:30 — US PPI** for October.
- **Around Nov 17–18 — Nvidia earnings** (date not yet confirmed by Nvidia). After a 9% drop
  in one day, this is the AI trade's next test, and crypto will follow it.
- **Dec 8–9 — Fed meeting.** Decision Dec 9 (US time). Market odds of a hike: ~75–80%.
- **Jan 3, 2027 — new Congress sworn in.** The CLARITY Act has to start over; watch who
  gets the Senate Banking gavel.
- **Daily:** Farside ETF flows. Two or three inflow days in a row would be the first sign
  the big buyers are back.

Bottom line: meme coins didn't die because of a meme. They bled because the whole risk
ladder got shaken, and they sit on the bottom rung. Watch the macro calendar before you
watch the trending page.
