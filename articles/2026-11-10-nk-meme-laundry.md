# North Korea is printing meme coins to wash the biggest heist in crypto history

## Hook

On a normal day, a brand-new meme coin with a cartoon logo and a joke ticker is just noise.
Between November 5 and 7, six of them on the NEAR blockchain were something else: a
laundry. According to blockchain analysts Elliptic, wallets tied to the $6.4 billion
Binance theft minted the tokens, bought them with stolen coins, and sold them back to
themselves. Each launch cost about $90.

The meme coin machine, built so anyone can launch a token in a minute, is now a tool of a
sanctioned state. That is today's story.

## What happened (numbers)

- **The theft.** On October 29, hackers drained Binance hot wallets across ten blockchains.
  Binance puts the loss at about $6.34 billion; TRM Labs says $6.36 billion; Elliptic
  $6.43 billion. Every count makes it the largest crypto theft on record, more than four
  times the roughly $1.5 billion Bybit theft of February 2025.
- **How they got in.** Binance says the attack began five months earlier, with a fake job
  interview: an engineer received a "coding test" carrying malware, and a tainted download
  gave the attackers a foothold. Co-CEO Richard Teng described the chain on November 3.
- **Who.** On October 31 the FBI, with the Secret Service and IRS-CI, named North Korea's
  TraderTraitor group. Kim Yo Jong denied it on November 3.
- **The freeze.** Tether and Circle froze more than $2.3 billion in about an hour. Elliptic
  counts $2.85 billion frozen or seized, 44% of the haul.
- **The rest.** Elliptic says about $3.11 billion still sits in theft-linked addresses,
  mostly ETH and bitcoin, "very likely" still held by North Korea.
- **The meme coins.** Six NEAR tokens (names included FUK, MEILI and SINU; logos showed
  animals and an "Elon 2028 President" image of Elon Musk, who has no link to them) moved
  about 117,000 NEAR, roughly $430,000. Laundering wallets made every sale of all six
  tokens and bought every unit of five of them. About 11.8 million NEAR (~$43.7 million)
  in the main stolen-NEAR wallet had not moved.

The $430,000 is under 0.01% of the theft. The bigger number is on Tron: TRM Labs
describes a self-minted token where the creator sent 300 million tokens to a seller
wallet, which sold them the next minute and withdrew nearly $100 million in receipt
tokens. After the Bybit theft, Elliptic traced at least $24 million through a homemade
Tron token. The trick is not new. It is getting bigger.

## Why it happened (the chain)

**1. Freezes pushed the money to chains nobody can freeze.** Stablecoins have an issuer
who can press stop, and Tether and Circle did, within minutes. Bitcoin, XRP, Solana and
NEAR have no such button, and coverage of the freezes notes nothing was frozen on those chains. So the
launderers do their work there.

**2. A self-made token turns stolen coins into "trading profits".** Mint a token, open a
pool against NEAR or a stablecoin, buy with stolen funds from wallet set A, sell from
wallet set B. On-chain, wallet B now holds coins that look like they came from a lucky
meme trade, not from Binance. It is the same buy-and-sell-to-yourself pattern that
on-chain sleuths flag in insider meme launches every week, used for a different goal.

**3. Scale needs automation.** Elliptic counted more than 20,000 wallets on 16
blockchains, at least 36 services and 65,000+ transactions by November 7: about one new
wallet every 25–30 seconds. Co-founder Tom Robinson called it laundering "on autopilot",
automated with human oversight, not an AI agent.

**4. Bridges did the heavy lifting, and politics is catching up.** In the first 72 hours
THORChain processed more than $46 million of the stolen funds, per Chainalysis. The FBI
asked node operators, swap platforms and DeFi services to block listed addresses.
THORChain's front end blocked them; its co-founder made no promise for node operators.
An unrelated THORChain halt on October 31, triggered by an Ethereum reorg, delayed the
laundering until swaps resumed on November 3. RUNE fell about 18–20% in that window.

**5. The sanctions stakes keep rising.** TRM Labs counts $12.8 billion stolen by North
Korea since 2017, $7.4 billion of it in 2026 alone. Its share of all crypto hack value
rose from 39% in 2024 to 63% in 2025 and 85% this year (Chainalysis says ~89%). In
Washington, Democrats won the Senate, and Elizabeth Warren is expected (not yet
confirmed) to chair the Senate Banking Committee from January, with subpoena power. She
has already pushed probes of the Trump family's crypto ventures and the TRUMP meme coin.
A state laundering billions through meme launches and bridges hands her a ready-made case.

## What on-chain data shows

- **Freezable vs. not.** $2.85 billion stuck, almost all of it stablecoins. The
  unfreezable $3.11 billion is what the launderers are working through, slowly.
- **The homemade tokens have no real buyers.** On NEAR, the launderers were both sides of
  every trade. Anyone tracking "new token, volume rising" sees fake demand.
- **Market mood stayed weak.** Spot bitcoin ETFs lost about $1.28 billion in the first
  week of November, bitcoin hit about $73,500 on November 8 (lowest since August), and
  stablecoin supply fell $4.68 billion last week per Lookonchain, about $21 billion below
  its May peak. The hack did not cause all of that, but it hit a market already short of
  fresh money.

## Who won, who lost

**Lost:** Binance, which says it set aside more than $6.34 billion and used about half of
its SAFU emergency fund to make users whole. RUNE holders, as THORChain became the face of
the laundering route. Meme traders on small chains, who now share pools with state
launderers and can't tell them apart.

**Won (for now):** the launderers, who still control about $3.11 billion. Tether and
Circle showed that freezing works fast, which also reminds every holder that their
stablecoins can be frozen.

**The same week, a different trench lesson:** Bubblemaps alleged that Solana trader Cupsey
pulled about $1.74 million through some 30 linked wallets that sold into copy traders
after his public buys. That is an allegation; we found no reply from him. The mechanism,
your own wallets on both sides of a trade, is the same one North Korea uses. The meme
launch machine does not check who is buying.
