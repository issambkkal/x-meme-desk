# Asia caught its chip crash. Nobody catches crypto.

Last week the AI chip trade broke, and it broke hardest in Asia. Within 72 hours Taiwan
opened its NT$500 billion rescue fund and cut short selling by 90%. Korea's state pension
fund bought into a circuit-breaker halt. And on Monday Japan confirmed it spent ¥15.4
trillion propping up the yen this summer.

Crypto took the same shock and got nothing like that. What it got was margin calls: more
than $1 billion in liquidations in a day, and bitcoin at its lowest since August.

That difference is the story. When stocks fall, governments have tools and a reason to
use them. Crypto has no lender, no fund and no short-selling ban. The only "backstop" is
someone else's forced selling.

## What happened

**Thursday, Nov 5 (US).** The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell 12.25%, its worst day
since March 2020 (NBC, Benzinga). The Nasdaq 100 lost 6.4%. Nvidia dropped 9.26%.
Analysts tied it to the midterms: Democrats won both chambers, and the market priced in
slower AI data-center building (Fortune, NBC). Benzinga counted 28 of the 30 SOXX
holdings at least 20% below their highs.

**Friday, Nov 6 (Asia).** The selling arrived in Seoul and Taipei.

- Korea's Kospi fell 8.55% to 4,923.47 by 9:03am and tripped a Level 1 circuit breaker,
  halting trade for 20 minutes (Asia Business Daily, Xinhua). SK Hynix fell more than 10%.
  After the restart, the National Pension Service and local institutions bought, and the
  index closed back above 5,000 (TradingKey).
- Taiwan's Taiex closed down 4.51% at 39,701.64, after a record intraday drop of 3,737.67
  points (Focus Taiwan, Taipei Times). For the week it lost 12.19%, its worst week since
  2001, and it sat 20.55% below its Oct 6 peak of 49,968.92: a bear market.

**Friday, Nov 6 (US).** A bounce. Nvidia rose 2.78% to $186.64, still its fifth losing week
in a row and about 22% below its October record of $239.24 (24/7 Wall St., Stock
Analysis). After the close its board opened an independent probe into reports that CEO
Jensen Huang was the target of a Chinese intelligence effort; Huang admitted an affair
with the woman involved. The board said it had no reason to believe company secrets were
compromised; Beijing called the claims "groundless" (CNN, CBS, TechCrunch, NPR). On
Polymarket, traders put 13% on Huang being removed.

**Sunday night to Monday, Nov 8–9 (crypto).** Bitcoin fell to about $73,761, its lowest
since August, before bouncing above $75,000 (CoinDesk). That is about 9% below the
$81,004 high of Nov 4.

## Why it happened: the chain

1. **Politics hit the AI build-out.** The midterm result made investors doubt the pace of
   US data-center spending (Fortune, NBC). Chips are the purest bet on that spending.
2. **Asia is the chip market.** TSMC is roughly 40% of the Taiex. Samsung and SK Hynix are
   about half of the Kospi. So a US chip crash becomes an Asian index crash overnight.
3. **Foreigners were already leaving.** Bloomberg-compiled data show foreign investors sold
   more than $45 billion of Taiwanese shares this quarter. October alone saw a record
   NT$994.3 billion (about $30 billion) of net foreign selling (Startup Fortune, BigGo).
4. **Then the states stepped in.** Taiwan's Financial Stabilization Fund authorised its
   10th intervention since it was created in 2000 (Focus Taiwan, bne IntelliNews). The
   regulator cut the daily cap on short sales from 30% of average volume to 3%, raised the
   short-sale margin from 90% to 130% from Nov 9, and told companies to buy back stock.
   Korea's pension money bought the halt. Japan's finance ministry said on Monday it spent
   ¥15.4 trillion (about $97 billion) on July 30, July 31 and Aug 3, including a record
   ¥7.98 trillion in one day and the first joint operation with the US since 1998 (Japan
   Times, Xinhua).
5. **It worked, for now.** On Monday the Taiex rose as much as 5% and TSMC about 7%
   (Benzinga, BigGo).
6. **Crypto had the same risk but none of the tools.** Bitcoin has been trading with tech.
   It fell with chips, then took two more hits over the weekend: the Nvidia probe and
   failed US-Iran talks. Marco Rubio called Iran's demands "outrageous"; Iran's foreign
   minister said there was "no progress" (CoinDesk, Reuters/Zerohedge, Al Jazeera).

And the Fed is not coming to help either. After October's +165,000 jobs report, CME
FedWatch put the chance of a December **hike** at about 77–81% (Yahoo Finance, Reuters).
The Fed meets Dec 8–9.

## What on-chain and market data show

- **Liquidations.** CoinDesk, citing CoinGlass, counted more than $1 billion liquidated in
  24 hours on Monday, with ether ($448 million) hit almost twice as hard as bitcoin ($272
  million). BeInCrypto's CoinGlass snapshot showed $855.7 million, over 140,000 traders,
  90.2% of it longs. The totals differ by time window; both say longs paid.
- **The biggest single victim.** Per EmberCN, a whale holding over $250 million in longs
  was liquidated around 6am: about 63,000 ETH (~$135 million) and 60.8 BTC. Reported loss:
  roughly $49–61 million depending on how it is counted (EmberCN, ChainCatcher via KuCoin).
- **ETFs.** US spot bitcoin ETFs lost about $1.28 billion in net outflows from Nov 2 to 6
  (Farside data via Mitrade and CryptoTicker). Nov 5: −$430.2 million. Nov 6: −$411.9
  million, of which BlackRock's IBIT was about −$403 million.
- **Sentiment.** The alternative.me Fear & Greed Index hit 25 on Nov 7, "extreme fear",
  its first such reading since Aug 6 (CryptoTicker).
- **Stablecoins.** Total supply is around $298–305 billion, below the May record of
  $315–322 billion depending on the tracker (Spark, Satoshi Macro). New dollars are not
  arriving to buy the dip.
- **Meme coins.** The trenches have been draining for weeks: Pump.fun earned about $7.5
  million in fees Nov 1–6, against $62.4 million in all of October (DefiLlama). PEPE, the
  biggest frog, is worth about $2 billion, a fraction of its peak (Coinbase).

## Who won, who lost

**Won (so far):** anyone who bought TSMC or the Taiex on Friday's close, with the state
fund behind them. Shorts on crypto perps, who collected the liquidations. People sitting in
cash, which pays close to the Fed's 3.75–4.00% while they wait.

**Lost:** leveraged crypto longs, more than 140,000 of them in one day. Taiwan short
sellers, whose daily limit was cut by 90%. Bitcoin miners and AI-linked stocks, which sit at the
meeting point of both crashes. And the "digital gold" argument: in this selloff bitcoin
behaved like a chip stock with no circuit breaker.

The honest counterpoint: rescues do not change the reason for the selling. Taiwan's fund
has stepped in nine times before, and short-sale bans tend to delay price discovery, not
cancel it. If the AI spending story keeps weakening, Asian indexes can fall again with the
fund inside them. Crypto, at least, finds its price fast.

## What to watch next

- **Tue Nov 10:** US October CPI, 8:30am ET (16:30 Doha). TSMC October revenue.
- **~Fri Nov 13:** Bitcoin difficulty adjustment.
- **Tue Nov 17:** Nvidia Q3 results after the US close (01:00 Doha, Nov 18), the first
  since the board probe.
- **Dec 8–9:** FOMC. Markets price a hike from 3.75–4.00% to 4.00–4.25%.
- **Ongoing:** how long Taiwan keeps the short-sale curbs, and whether foreign selling
  in Taipei and Seoul slows. If Asia stabilises and bitcoin doesn't, the link is broken
  and crypto has its own problem.
