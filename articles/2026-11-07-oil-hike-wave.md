# The $100 oil problem: why the world's central banks are hiking and meme coins pay first

On Thursday the Bank of England raised interest rates for the first time since 2023. The
reason it gave was not wages or house prices. It was energy, pushed up by the war in the
Middle East.

It is not alone. The US Fed, the European Central Bank and the Bank of Japan have all
hiked since September, and markets expect most of them to hike again in December. In 2026
the world is paying for oil with higher interest rates, and crypto, which needs cheap
money more than almost anything else, is paying with it. Meme coins, as usual, pay first.

## What happened (the numbers)

**Oil is back near $100.** Brent crude touched about $100 on Nov 6 after Iran's
Revolutionary Guard said more tankers had hit mines in the Strait of Hormuz (HDFC Sky,
Investing.com). Trading Economics puts Brent about 54% above a year ago. Before the war
began on Feb 28, Brent was near $73.50; it went above $120 in early March (Oanda).

**The tanker claims are not confirmed.** No maritime authority confirmed the Nov 5–6
attacks, and President Trump accused Iran of making them up to lift prices (Iran
International, Al Jazeera). That doesn't matter much to traders: Iran declared the strait
closed again on Oct 26, and Polymarket traders gave only about a 7% chance that Hormuz
traffic returns to normal by year end (Polymarket, Nov 2).

**Energy is now the inflation story.**
- US: consumer prices rose 3.7% in the year to September, and 0.6% in the month alone.
  Gasoline jumped 9.1% in September and 34.2% over the year, more than half of the monthly
  rise (BLS, Fox Business). Core inflation, without food and energy, was 2.6%.
- At the pump: the AAA national average was about $4.07 on Nov 6, against about $3.08 a
  year earlier, the highest ever for this time of year (AAA).
- Euro area: inflation hit 4.0% in October, a three-year high, with energy up 20.8%
  (Eurostat flash).

**And the central banks are answering with hikes.**
- **Bank of England:** up 0.25 points to 4.00% on Nov 5, an 8–1 vote, with one member
  wanting 4.25%. It expects inflation of about 5% by mid-2027 (Bank of England).
- **Fed:** 3.75–4.00% after a September hike, its first in about three years (PBS). On
  Nov 6, New York Fed president John Williams called inflation "absolutely too high" (The
  Hill). After Friday's strong jobs report, market odds of another hike on Dec 9 sat
  around 70–80%, depending on the gauge (Invezz, CME FedWatch).
- **ECB:** deposit rate 2.50% after a September hike (CNBC). On Nov 3, Bundesbank chief
  Joachim Nagel said a December hike was "very likely necessary" (FXStreet, Newsquawk).
- **Bank of Japan:** 1.25% after a September hike; held on Oct 30 in an 8–1 vote, with one
  member wanting 1.50% now (FXStreet, Nikkei).

## Why it matters (the chain)

The chain is short and it runs one way:

**Hormuz closed → oil up → gasoline and power bills up → headline inflation up → central
banks hike → money costs more → speculative assets get sold.**

Central banks usually try to "look through" an oil shock, because rates can't pump more
oil. This time they are not, because the shock has lasted eight months and they fear it
leaks into wages and prices of everything else. That is exactly what the Bank of England
said it was trying to stop.

Crypto sits at the far end of that chain. Bitcoin has traded more like a tech stock than
like "digital gold" through this war, so it reacts to rates, not to headlines about
safety. You could see it this week: on Nov 4, when oil fell on hopes of a US–Iran deal,
Bitcoin was around $80,000 (CryptoTicker). By Nov 6, after the tanker claims, the jobs
report and a tech selloff, it was around $75,400–75,700 (CoinDesk, Coinbase).

## What the on-chain and flow data shows

The money that crypto runs on is leaving.

- **ETFs:** US spot Bitcoin ETFs took in $25.6 billion in 2026 and paid out $26.1 billion,
  leaving the year about $502 million negative as of Nov 5. Their assets fell to $96.65
  billion from $113.29 billion at the end of 2025, a drop of about 15% (SoSoValue data via
  BeInCrypto and KuCoin).
- **Stablecoins:** the total supply of dollar stablecoins was about $291.6 billion on
  Nov 6, down about 2% in a week and 4.6% in 30 days (DefiLlama, CoinTurk). Stablecoins are
  the cash that buys crypto, and that cash pile is shrinking.
- **Leverage:** about $1.08 billion of positions were liquidated in the Nov 5 selloff,
  $954 million of them longs (CoinGlass data via crypto.news). The Fear & Greed index fell
  to 28.
- **Meme coin activity:** Pump.fun earned about $7.54 million in fees in Nov 1–6, roughly
  $1.26 million a day, against about $2.0 million a day in October, so about 37% lower
  (DefiLlama, our math).

One counterpoint: Circle minted about $750 million of new USDC on Solana in a single day
this week (SolanaFloor). Some cash is being parked on-chain. Parked is not spent, though.

## Who won, who lost

**Lost:** leveraged longs, wiped out in one afternoon. Meme coin holders: by a recent
BeInCrypto tally, Dogecoin sits about 90% below its high and the Trump and Melania coins
98–99% below theirs, while the S&P 500 is only about 3% off its record. CryptoQuant data
reported by CryptoPotato showed about $1.4 billion of meme coin sell pressure on Binance in
October, with WIF down over 10% and BONK and FLOKI over 15% in one week. Anyone who bought
Bitcoin ETFs this year is, on aggregate, underwater on flows.

**Won (or at least held up):** holders of cash, who now get paid 4% to wait. The dollar,
which rose about 0.6% after Friday's jobs data (ActionForex). And the casino itself: even with volumes down,
Pump.fun has about $322 million of 2026 revenue, second only to Hyperliquid among crypto
projects (CoinGecko-based research via The Currency Analytics). The platform earns on every
trade, win or lose.

The lesson isn't "sell meme coins". It's that meme coins are the last link in a chain that
starts at a single shipping lane in the Gulf. When rates go up because of oil, the most speculative money
leaves first and comes back last.

## What to watch next (Doha time)

- **Tue Nov 10, 16:30:** US CPI for October. No year-on-year figure is expected because
  the October 2025 data is missing after last year's shutdown (Morningstar, RBC); watch the
  monthly number. RBC expects +0.3% for headline and core. Gas fell in October, so a soft
  print is possible; a hot core number would cement a December hike.
- **Fri Nov 13, 16:30:** US PPI.
- **Wed Nov 18:** final euro area inflation for October (Eurostat).
- **Any day:** Hormuz. A real reopening deal is the single biggest upside risk for crypto
  in this chain. Polymarket says about 7%.
- **Dec 9, 22:00:** Fed decision. **Dec 17:** ECB and Bank of England. **Dec 18:** Bank of
  Japan; more than 80% of economists polled expect a hike to 1.50% (FXStreet).
